# My Writing Manifesto

I want to write and publish fiction - I have done since I was about 11. But for many reasons, I struggle to do it.



I don’t lack the ability or discipline - I published a 100,000 nonfiction book on top of my regular work while I was an academic.  I would estimate I’ve published nearly a million words in the last 15 years.



I have a child, and a different career now, and I find myself struggling to summon the energy to write regularly.  I need to find a way to unblock myself, develop better writing habits, and start discovering the fiction I really want to write. I'm writing this post to hold myself accountable in a public forum, and get me back into writing.



## What is stopping me from writing now?



- I have several ideas that are underdeveloped, and I don’t know which one to tackle first.
- I’m too tired at the end of a working day
- I barely read anymore, let alone write
- I feel guilty/selfish about spending lots of time working in isolation
- I’m not confident in my ideas (partly because I don’t fully understand them)
- I don’t have a network of people who share the same interest
- I struggle to make/participate in creative writing groups.
- I am not held accountable for my lack of writing



## What are my (fiction) writing weaknesses?



- I focus too hard on plot over character
- I don’t spend enough time living with the characters I write, and finding ways for the characters to drive the plot.
- I get bored of an idea easily - probably because it’s underdeveloped, or I don’t know what the story is actually trying to say, i.e. what are its major themes?
- I’m not good at finishing fiction.



## How did I finish a 100,000 word textbook?



- I had a hard deadline
- I had a solid outline
- Research counted as work
- I knew roughly how many words I had to write, and what I hadn’t written yet
- I had regular writing sessions (at least once a week)
- I learned to write “in the gaps” - I would type out a few hundred words or so on my phone or tablet on long winding bus commutes from Ladybank to St Andrews
- I spent a lot of time writing in the University’s Main Library/Reading Rooms, offering peace and quiet.



## How am I going to develop a good fiction writing habit?



- I’m going to ease into it by committing to one writing session per week, and a weekly word count target of 100 words.
- I’m going to use my home office as a quiet place
- I’m going to use some basic tools (e.g. Google Forms) to help me track word counts and writing time, and make me more accountable to others.  I did this for the textbook, and found it encouraging!
- I’m not going to stress about what I am writing about.  If I’m driven to write on project Y instead of project X, I will write on project Y.  I am under no obligation to finish either project first.
- My word count will not just apply to the main text, but character sketches, plotting, research, etc.
- I am going to use Scrivener as my main writing tool, but I am going to relearn “writing in the gaps” using Evernote and my phone/iPad if I have to.



## How am I going to develop my skills as a writer?



- I’m going to spend more time mapping out all of my ideas first.  By having a well curated set of ideas/outlines/prompts, I will find it easy to write something.
- I’m going to take a writing class.  The class will count towards my time and word count goals.
- I’m going to find people to share drafts of my work with (that I’m not closely related to).  I will return the favour by reading other people’s drafts.
- I’m going to reread all my old stories, and start examining them critically for insights.
- I’m going to keep a journal (on this blog!)  Journal entries will count towards my time and word goals
- I’m going to spend more time reading generally, and less time on social media.
- I’m going to protect my free time more aggressively, and not let work encroach into it.



\
I’ve already done a few of the things above. I reread my old stories (watch out for a future post on them). I also joined a local creative writing group, and I’m going to polish up some material for a critique session.



I will also be trying to write regularly on here, to keep words coming out of my keyboard. The posts will be mostly for my benefit, but I hope you enjoy reading them too.
