# A Dysonesque Gravitational Wave Generator

*PSA: this blogpost contains a good deal of spitballing, and not a lot of calculations. It’s an idea I’d love to see fleshed out, but it would take more knowledge (and time) than I possess at the moment.*



With the recent advances in gravitational wave (GW) detection since LIGO’s initial discoveries, the idea of gravitational wave data being searched for signs of intelligent signalling has continued to grow.



But how could an extraterrestrial intelligence (ETI) produce ripples in spacetime that are detectable at interstellar distances? The GWs we have detected are compact merger events, the meeting of enormous masses. It seems that to generate GWs that humans can see, you need to either move lots of matter, or find ways to make it resonate.



I’ve had a thought tumbling around my head for about five years, about how ETIs could build a GW generator. I don’t have the general relativity background to calculate the generator’s output amplitude, so who knows how detectable this would be, but let’s write down the idea.



The basic concept revolves around Dyson spheres. These are massive shells, with a radius comparable to the Earth’s orbit. Either complete or composed of multiple pieces, a Dyson sphere encompasses a star to absorb its radiation (and to potentially provide an artificial habitat).



The concept has been tweaked in various directions. Concentric shells provide a thermodynamic engine that could be turned to computation (Matrioshka Brains). Partial shells provide an imbalance between radiation pressure and gravity, resulting in net thrust to push the star (Shkadov Thrusters).



My idea belongs to this category of stellar engine. Imagine a sphere that is composed of many pieces in a mosaic. Each piece is composed of a reflective side, and an absorptive side. Now imagine that each piece is free to rotate, showing the different sides to the star. With careful control of each piece, one could change the radiative equilibrium on specific parts of the star at will. In effect, one could apply a highly tuned variable force to the star, playing it like a musical instrument.



If this is a strong enough force (or one can find a resonance), the star will undergo perturbations that should produce gravitational waves.



**How powerful would these waves be?** No idea. I’d love to find out. Maybe a little laser activity would be needed to pep up the process.



**What would be the characteristic GW frequency?** The upper limit would be linked to the various crossing timescales in the star, as well as the asteroseismic timescales, so I’m guessing it’s a much lower frequency than those seen so far.



**Would it be focused?** That depends on the skill of the musician playing the star. I can’t imagine it to be a very collimated signal though.



**Would there be an electromagnetic counterpart?** Oh yes. What a weird asteroseismic signal it would be!



**What polarisation would the waves have?** Buggered if I know.



**What would we call it?**\
The Forgan Instrument, naturally. Unless someone else has already thought of it.
