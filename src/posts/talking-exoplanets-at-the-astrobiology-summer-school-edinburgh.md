# Talking Exoplanets at the Astrobiology Summer School (Edinburgh)

It was a great pleasure to be invited to lecture at the Astrobiology Society of Britain's Summer School this week at the University of Edinburgh.  I really enjoyed getting to know the latest crop of UK PhD students studying a range of topics related to Life in the Universe, from engineering to microbiology to glaciology.
I learned a great deal from the lectures given by my co-presenters, Frances Butcher and Mark Fox-Powell, on the prospects for life on Mars and the icy moons of Jupiter and Saturn.  It's clear that there are big things on the way for astrobiology missions targeting lots of our neighbours!
I was there to give an introduction to exoplanet detection, and the search for Life beyond the solar system.  It's always good to remind the astrobiology community, who are rightly focused on our neighbouring planets, that there are thousands of worlds out there that can teach us about how habitable environments are formed and destroyed.  However, while solar system scientists know lots about a few planets, exoplanet scientists know almost nothing about thousands, so the challenge is very different!
The afternoon session saw the students discuss some of the more basic demands of research life - how to write a paper, create a poster, manage one's time, collaborators and supervisors, and how to speak about one's research.  I was really impressed by the ideas and clear thinking coming out of these groups (and happy to lend my advice where it was needed).
All in all, a very satisfying day, and a lovely excuse to visit my alma mater (even if only briefly).
