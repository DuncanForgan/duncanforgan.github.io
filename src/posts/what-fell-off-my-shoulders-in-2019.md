# What fell off my shoulders in 2019

I have an urge to write a review of the year (probably because I wrote so many for [Research the Headlines](https://duncanforgan.wordpress.com/2013/08/31/young-academy-of-scotland-launches-research-the-headlines/) over the years). This isn't really a review, more another cathartic blerp of words like [my last post.](https://duncanforgan.wordpress.com/2019/03/25/on-leaving-academia/)
Spotify encouraged me by reminding me of the music I listened to this year. Seeing that track list propelled me back into May, when I burst into tears after finishing this run:
![](/images/posts/what-fell-off-my-shoulders-in-2019/img_1178-1.png)
It was the first 5k I had run (since quitting academia) where I felt like part of my old fitness had come back. I felt 25 again.
With a hammerblow, I remembered that I published my first journal article when I was 25. Ten years of writing papers had stunted my health. What else had I lost?
As I tasted the bitterness of this thought, a decade sloughed off my shoulders and onto the cherry blossom at my feet, and I began to weep.
(The songs were the first three from Bear's Den's "so that you might hear me".)
Academia didn't steal my health. I gave it away freely. In a dim, unconscious sort of way I proffered up my health and happiness as part of a transaction for a permanent position. Little did I realise that academia did not value these items one iota, and that plenty others had not made this pointless sacrifice and still found success.
As I gasped for breath, wiping away tears to the lyrics of "Finding Bottles", I realised with deep anger that I had been an idiot for giving away the wrong bits of myself, and simultaneous deep relief that I had stopped doing that.
It was that relief that allowed whatever it was to slip from my shoulders, like a snake's shedded skin. I'm still not sure what that burden truly was, but I know that I don't have to lift it again.
I end this year in a far better place than I began it. For the first time in years, I face a new year (and a new decade) without deep existential career angst, and guilt about putting my family's life on hold.
2019 has been difficult at times, and wonderful at others. At times, I still miss my old life. I'm hugely grateful that I've started my new career with an [incomparable company](http://jobs.airts.co.uk/) that values me in a way a university never could. My ideas are being listened to, and actioned (gasp). I'm surrounded by lovely, intelligent, dynamic work colleagues, joined by a single purpose, a true collaboration without any undercutting by internal funding battles.
And I'm back in my favourite city, with my family all living and working there too, in a house big enough to fit us. Who knew that was even *allowed*?
I hope you all find your path in 2020, wherever that may take you. And be free to slip that weight from your shoulders as you go.
